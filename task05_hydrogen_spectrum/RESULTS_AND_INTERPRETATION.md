## Main conclusion

Hydrogen's bound-state energies are discrete, so its emitted photon energies
and wavelengths are also discrete. For the stationary-nucleus Bohr model,

$$
E_n=-\frac{E_{\mathrm R}}{n^2},
$$

and a downward level change $n_i\rightarrow n_f$ releases

$$
E_\gamma=E_{\mathrm R}
\left(\frac{1}{n_f^2}-\frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)>0.
$$

Every calculated point lies on the photon relation

$$
E_\gamma=hf=\frac{hc}{\lambda},
$$

but only integer level differences are included. The dashed inverse curve in
the main graph is therefore a physical guide, not a continuous hydrogen
emission spectrum.

## Model and constants

The implementation uses exact SI values for $h$, $c$, and $e$, together with
the 2022 CODATA central value

$$
R_\infty=10\,973\,731.568157\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}.
$$

This gives

$$
E_{\mathrm R}=hcR_\infty=13.6056931229905\ \mathrm{eV}
$$

and

$$
hc=1239.84198433200\ \mathrm{eV\,nm}.
$$

The calculation deliberately uses $R_\infty$, corresponding to an infinitely
massive stationary nucleus. It is therefore internally consistent with the
ideal equation in the challenge material. A measured hydrogen spectrum uses a
reduced-mass Rydberg constant and contains further small corrections.

## Interpretation of the required graph

The graph uses photon energy on the vertical axis and vacuum wavelength on a
logarithmic horizontal axis. The log scale is necessary because this one
catalogue spans approximately $92\ \mathrm{nm}$ to $38.8\ \mu\mathrm m$.

Short-wavelength Lyman photons carry the most energy. Balmer points occupy the
near-ultraviolet and visible range. Transitions ending at $n_f\geq3$ are
infrared because their level differences are smaller. The inverse
$E_\gamma\propto1/\lambda$ trend is exact for every photon, while the clustering
of markers shows that the atom supplies only particular energy differences.

The hollow diamonds are analytical $n_i\rightarrow\infty$ series limits. They
are not additional finite transitions. They show where increasingly high
initial levels crowd together.

## Series convergence

For fixed $n_f$, increasing $n_i$ reduces $1/n_i^2$ toward zero. The photon
energy therefore approaches $E_{\mathrm R}/n_f^2$, and the wavelength
approaches

$$
\lambda_\infty=\frac{n_f^2}{R_\infty}.
$$

The first five limits are:

| Series | $n_f$ | Limit energy / eV | Limit wavelength / nm |
| --- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lyman | 1 | 13.605693 | 91.126705 |
| Balmer | 2 | 3.401423 | 364.506820 |
| Paschen | 3 | 1.511744 | 820.140346 |
| Brackett | 4 | 0.850356 | 1458.027281 |
| Pfund | 5 | 0.544228 | 2278.167626 |

The convergence figure confirms that each finite series approaches its own
dashed analytical limit monotonically. The Balmer limit lies in the
near-ultraviolet, which explains why high Balmer lines crowd toward the violet
edge and then leave the declared visible band.

## Assumptions and limitations

### Physical assumptions

- The proton is stationary and infinitely massive.
- Only the ideal non-relativistic Bohr energy formula is used.
- Emission photon energy equals the positive atomic energy loss.
- Wavelengths are vacuum wavelengths.
- The finite catalogue stops at $n=10$ while series limits are analytical.

### Effects not modelled

- electron--proton reduced mass;
- fine structure, hyperfine structure, and the Lamb shift;
- Zeeman and Stark splitting;
- transition probabilities, degeneracy, or electric-dipole selection rules;
- natural, Doppler, pressure, or instrumental linewidths; and
- experimental calibration or uncertainty.

Consequently, the 45 entries are a complete set of declared level-energy
differences in the finite Bohr study, not a prediction that all lines have
equal strength or are equally observable.

### Numerical and visual limitations

- Calculations use double-precision floating-point arrays after constants are
  frozen from decimal source values.
- Many stored digits support reproducibility; they do not represent
  experimental precision.
- Marker position is quantitative, but marker size and colour are explanatory
  encodings.
- The visible-band shading is a declared convention, not a measured response
  function for the human eye.
- Connecting lines on the convergence graph organize each series; they are not
  additional atomic states.

## What the validation proves and does not prove

The validation establishes that the implementation agrees with the frozen
ideal equations, constants, units, transition domain, identities, named
anchors, and series limits. It strongly guards against sign, unit, ordering,
omission, duplication, and coordinate-conversion errors.

It does not experimentally validate the Bohr model or show that omitted
real-hydrogen corrections are negligible for precision spectroscopy. Model
verification and experimental validation are different claims.
