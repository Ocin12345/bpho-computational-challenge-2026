# Master Narration — Tasks 1–10

This is the final narration for the ten-slide, three-minute BPhO presentation.
It contains 410 spoken words. At 150 words per minute it lasts approximately
164 seconds, leaving about 16 seconds for slide changes, emphasis and a natural
closing pause.

## Task 1 — Two-dimensional random walk

> Task 1 samples fixed-length steps at uniformly distributed angles. Fifty
> paths show no preferred direction. Four hundred thousand independent walks
> give an MSD slope of 0.9992, matching the predicted N s squared and strongly
> confirming an unbiased, isotropic model.

Cue: begin on the path ensemble, move to the endpoint cloud, and finish on the
MSD result card.

## Task 2 — Collision-driven Brownian motion

> Task 2 replaces prescribed steps with collisions. One thousand small
> particles drive a larger tracer along an irregular path. Across sixty-four
> runs, endpoints are unbiased and MSD is linear: R squared 0.983, with
> diffusion coefficient 2.25 times ten to the minus three.

Cue: show the tracer scene, then the linear MSD region and endpoint mean.

## Task 3 — Planck radiation and Einstein heat capacity

> Task 3 moves to thermal quantum physics. Higher temperature shifts the
> black-body peak to shorter wavelength while total emission follows T to the
> fourth. Einstein temperatures set how seven solids approach the three-R
> heat-capacity limit. All twenty-seven analytical and numerical checks pass.

Cue: compare the two upper plots, then close on the validation panels.

## Task 4 — Photoelectric effect

> Task 4 applies Einstein's photoelectric equation to nine metals. Every
> stopping-potential curve has gradient h over e; work function sets the
> threshold. Only sodium reaches visible light at 517 nanometres, and below
> threshold no photoelectrons exist. All forty-three checks pass.

Cue: follow frequency, wavelength and the explicit copper threshold in that
order.

## Task 5 — Hydrogen spectrum

> Task 5 uses ten Bohr levels to calculate all forty-five downward transitions.
> Photon energy follows h c over lambda, yet only discrete wavelengths occur.
> The Balmer series crosses visible light, including ideal H-alpha at 656
> nanometres. All thirty checks pass.

Cue: move from the energy-wavelength relation to the level diagram and visible
Balmer lines.

## Task 6 — Electron diffraction

> Task 6 turns waves into diffraction rings. Higher accelerating voltage
> shortens the electron de Broglie wavelength. Exact spherical geometry makes
> the required one-over-root-V graph linear and recovers both graphite
> spacings, 0.123 and 0.213 nanometres. All thirty-nine checks pass.

Cue: begin on the ring geometry and finish on the two recovered slopes.

## Task 7 — Particle in a box

> Task 7 confines an electron inside a one-nanometre box. Boundary conditions
> create standing waves and energies growing as n squared. Normalised
> probability densities and a ground-state uncertainty of 0.568 h-bar satisfy
> Heisenberg's bound. Thirty-seven independent checks pass.

Cue: follow probability density, quantised energies and uncertainty.

## Task 8 — Quantum mismatch calculator

> Task 8 compares classical Malus probabilities with entangled predictions. At
> minus and plus thirty degrees, classical mismatch is 37.5 percent, while
> quantum mismatch is 75 percent. The full sweep separates their angle
> dependence, and finite samples show counting fluctuations. Sixty-six checks
> pass.

Cue: state the selected-angle comparison, then use the sweep and sample panel
as evidence.

## Task 9 — Compton scattering

> Task 9 models exact Compton scattering at five energies. Fractional shift and
> electron speed rise with angle, while recoil angle falls. At 200
> kiloelectronvolts and 90 degrees, the results are 0.391, 0.434 c and 35.7
> degrees. Forty-four conservation checks pass.

Cue: trace the scattering geometry, then read the three angle-dependent
results.

## Task 10 — Hydrogenic orbitals

> Finally, Task 10 constructs normalised hydrogenic orbitals from S through G.
> Twenty-five real states and coloured-glass slices reveal density and nodes.
> Energy scales as Z squared over n squared, while size scales inversely with Z.
> All twenty-two scientific checks pass.

Cue: finish on the coloured-glass density, the S-through-G sequence and the
validation badge, then pause.
